# Zuniga Studio — Homepage Concept Walkthrough

*A cinematic, present-tense walkthrough of the homepage experience, header to footer. Intended to be read at a stakeholder pitch meeting before any Figma or Framer work begins. Companion to `BLUEPRINT.md`, which holds the strategy beneath this concept.*

---

## How to read this document

This is not a wireframe document. This is a description of an *experience* — the felt, watched, scrolled experience of arriving at Zuniga Studio for the first time. Read it as you'd read a screenplay, or a museum curator's walk-through of a new exhibition.

Every motion, color, and typographic choice referenced here is anchored in the Strategic Foundation of the blueprint. If you find yourself asking "but why this?", the answer is one section earlier in `BLUEPRINT.md`.

We are presenting a complete, intentional, premium digital experience — built specifically for Florida small-business owners who recognize quality when they see it and have been waiting for someone in their state to offer it to them.

## Frame I — Pre-Load (the first 300 milliseconds)

The visitor arrives. The page is **obsidian** — total, confident black, edge to edge. There is no spinner. There is no flicker. There is no white flash, no skeleton state, no hero image popping into place.

Instead, in the dead center of the obsidian field, a single thin **gold rule** — perhaps 80 pixels long, perhaps a hairline thick — appears and holds for a quarter-second. This is the wordmark resolving. Then, very quickly, the rule extends to a quiet two-line composition in **Cinzel** — `ZUNIGA · STUDIO` — sized small, ivory on obsidian, centered.

It holds for one full beat. The visitor's eye lands on it before they realize a page is loading.

Then the wordmark begins to migrate. It slides up and to the left, scaling down slightly, into its final resting place in the top-left of the nav bar. As it settles, the page beneath it dissolves into **ivory** — a warm, almost-paper off-white — and the hero arrives.

The entire pre-load takes 600–800 milliseconds. The visitor has, before consciously registering it, just watched a wordmark resolve into its home. The studio has announced itself.

## Frame II — The Opening Statement *(Hero)*

The page is now ivory. The visitor sees:

In the **top-left**, the wordmark rests in its persistent nav. Beside it, in body sans tracked-out small caps, the navigation items: `Work · Services · Approach · Studio · Journal`. At the far right of the nav: `Start a Project`, sized identically to the other links but with a thin gold underline. The bar is otherwise transparent. The obsidian wordmark and small-caps links sit cleanly on the ivory.

In the **left two-thirds of the viewport**, beginning roughly 12% from the left margin and breathing into the center, a colossal **Cinzel headline** begins to write itself. Letter by letter, each character masks in from below as if engraved into the page in real time. The headline is sized somewhere in the range of clamp(72px, 8vw, 144px), with a line-height of 0.95 — tight, monumental, almost architectural in its proportion. The total reveal takes about 1.2 seconds.

The headline is three to five lines long. It is declarative. It is not a question, not a sales line, not a value proposition phrased as a benefit. It is a statement about what the studio *believes about design*. (The exact words live in the copy doc; the typographic event is what matters here.)

Beneath the headline, a short supporting line in modern clean sans-serif fades in — 18–20 pixel size, maximum 480 pixels wide. This is where Florida and small-business context enter naturally, almost as a caption to the larger statement above.

Below that, two CTAs resolve in: **`Start a Project`** in solid gold with obsidian type, and **`See Selected Work`** outlined in obsidian, transparent fill. The gold CTA pulses once — a soft, almost imperceptible scale-in — as if it had been waiting for the visitor.

On the **right edge of the viewport**, there is no image. There is no photograph, no illustration, no animated character. There is one element only: a tall **vertical gold rule** running from roughly 30% to 70% of the viewport height, paired with a small Roman numeral **`I`** in Cinzel at its top. This signals: this is a publication. We are at Chapter One. Restraint is the message.

At the **very bottom of the viewport**, a thin meta strip in tiny small caps: `EST. 2025 · FLORIDA · DESIGN + STRATEGY`. Almost invisible, but it adds the felt-quality of a museum wall card.

A small downward chevron, also in obsidian, bobs gently in the bottom-center every four seconds. It disappears the moment the visitor scrolls.

The **cursor** — a small 12-pixel obsidian disc that has been quietly following the visitor's mouse since pre-load — expands into a thin ring as it crosses the CTA buttons. It scales 1.0 to 2.4 over 180ms, with a soft spring-physics trail of about 80ms. This is the only "responsive to you" cue in the hero. It is enough.

The visitor scrolls.

## Frame III — The Quiet Trust *(Identity Strip)*

The hero releases its grip on the viewport. The background shifts from ivory to **cream** — `#F7EAC4` — a warming layer, almost like turning the page from the title page of a book into the interior.

There is no announcement. There is no headline. There is just a single horizontal band of generous vertical padding (somewhere between 80 and 120 pixels top and bottom). On the left of the band, in small-caps body sans, a single label: `TRUSTED BY`. On the right, three to five client wordmarks in monochrome obsidian — never logos in color, never logos in motion, never more than five.

The marks fade in sequentially as the band enters the viewport, with a 60-millisecond stagger. They rest at 60% opacity. When the visitor hovers a single wordmark, it gains 100% opacity — and only that one. There is no carousel. There is no scroll. There is no "view all our clients." Just three to five names, quietly stated.

If, in the early days of the studio, there are not yet five recognizable client marks to display, this strip becomes a single line of meta evidence: a credential, an award, a press mention — something verifiable, named, and short. Honesty is the rule. The strip never inflates what isn't true.

The strip ends. The cream surface continues into the next frame.

## Frame IV — The Studio's Stance *(Manifesto)*

The visitor enters a wide, single-column block of text. The viewport is otherwise empty — extremely generous side margins, no decorative imagery, no flanking visuals. This is the section where the studio *speaks*.

At the top of the column, a **Cinzel pull statement** sized around 48 pixels — two to four lines, centered or left-aligned to grid. As the section enters view, the statement reveals word by word. Each word fades in with a 220-millisecond duration, staggered by 80 milliseconds. By the time the visitor finishes reading, the studio has stated a principle out loud.

Beneath the pull statement, a thin **gold rule** — a single horizontal hairline — has drawn itself in from left to right. It is the kind of detail a luxury press release uses to separate dateline from body.

Then come two or three short paragraphs in body sans, sized at a comfortable 18 pixels with relaxed line-height. The paragraphs fade in with a simple opacity-and-lift transition (12 pixels of upward translation, 500 milliseconds). Each paragraph is short — four to six lines maximum. The voice is measured. There are no buzzwords. There are no exclamation marks. The studio is taking a position.

Somewhere mid-paragraph, a faint **gold underline** writes itself beneath one chosen phrase — three or four words, no more — as the visitor dwells. It is the only emphasis in the section. The phrase chosen for this emphasis is the load-bearing belief of the studio. (Copy decides which.)

This section is where small-business owners who don't share the studio's values quietly close the tab. That is correct. The manifesto is the qualifier.

The visitor scrolls.

## Frame V — The Atlas of Offerings *(Services)*

A return to **ivory**. The viewport widens. The studio is about to show its work.

At the top-left of the section, in Cinzel sized at about 32 pixels, a single anchor heading: `Offerings`. To its right, a thin tracked-out small-caps tagline if needed; otherwise empty, restrained.

Below the heading, the **Services Atlas** opens. This is not a grid. This is an asymmetric editorial composition.

A single large **primary card** for Website Design & Redesign occupies the upper-left, sized roughly 60% of the available column width and about 480 pixels tall. The card has a hairline obsidian border, sharp 90-degree corners, and a cream interior tinted slightly warmer than the page background. Inside: a small Roman numeral `I` in the top-left corner. Below that: `Website Design & Redesign` in small Cinzel. Below that: two to three lines of body-sans description. Below that: a tight three-item bullet list in body sans (specific includes — strategy session, custom design, hand-built code). At the card's bottom-right: a per-card CTA, `Discuss this →`, in small obsidian text.

Surrounding the primary card, four supporting cards arrange themselves in a staircase pattern — `II. Local SEO`, `III. E-Commerce Websites`, `IV. Website Maintenance`, `V. Brand Identity & Logo Design`. They are smaller, but identical in structure. The Roman numerals create a numbered system across the page that reads, top to bottom, left to right, as a chapter list.

As the visitor scrolls into the section, the cards stagger in — 80 milliseconds between each — with a slight upward lift and opacity fade.

Then the interaction begins. The visitor's cursor enters the atlas. The disc is now followed by a subtle, soft **gold spotlight** — a 600-pixel radial gradient at very low opacity — that tracks the cursor across the grid. The atlas illuminates wherever attention falls. This is the "responding to you" interaction — quiet, never loud, but unmistakably alive.

When the cursor hovers a single service card:
- The card **lifts 4 pixels** with a soft 24-pixel shadow appearing beneath
- A **gold underline** draws itself under the service name
- A small **line-illustration icon** specific to the service emerges from behind the card's right edge — drawn in over 600 milliseconds, like an inked stamp completing itself
- The cursor disc changes into a small `→` indicator

When the visitor moves to the next card, the previous card releases — softly, in 240ms — back to rest.

At the bottom of the section, a small text link: `View All Services →`. It leads to a dedicated `/services` page where each offering gets its own room.

The visitor has now seen the whole offering — felt the depth of thinking per service, recognized that this studio treats its services as a system, not a list.

## Frame VI — The Work *(Selected Spotlights)*

The page tone shifts again. A return to **cream** — warmer than the services frame, signaling: now we show what we've done.

A single Cinzel anchor at the top-left: `Selected Work`. Beneath it, the spotlights begin.

The **first spotlight** is a wide editorial composition. On the **left 60% of the viewport**, a large client image — a real screenshot, presented inside a deliberate device mockup that feels intentional (not a floating screen — an architectural framing, like a magazine fold-out). The image is offset slightly from the page grid, breaking the column gutter on purpose.

On the **right 40%**, vertically aligned to the image:
- Client name in medium Cinzel (about 36 pixels)
- A category descriptor in small caps body sans (e.g., `Therapy Practice · West Palm Beach`)
- One outcome stat in large Cinzel (e.g., `+340%`) with a small descriptor below in body sans (e.g., `organic traffic, 6 months post-launch`)
- A brief list of three or four project tags, each tracked-out and quiet
- A single link: `View Case Study →`

As the visitor scrolls into the spotlight, the image **stamps into place** — a Framer Magic Motion move where the image animates from a smaller "stamped" position into its full frame. The outcome stat number counts up from zero to its final value over about 700 milliseconds.

When the visitor hovers the spotlight, the image gains a subtle 1.02 scale, and a small tagline reveals beneath the project title — `Read the full work →`. The cursor reads `View` in small caps.

The visitor scrolls. The second spotlight mirrors the first — but the imagery is on the **right 60%**, the text on the **left 40%**. The alternation creates rhythm. The visitor doesn't notice it consciously, but the page is breathing.

Two spotlights, well-executed, land harder than six. If a third spotlight is added, it returns to the left-image composition of the first. Beyond three, the spotlights move to a dedicated `/work` index.

At the close of the section: `View All Work →` — quiet, lower-right of the page.

## Frame VII — The Method *(Approach / The Process)*

The page transitions back to **ivory**. The viewport quiets again. The studio is about to show how it works.

The anchor at the top reads `The Process`. Below it, a single line of body sans introduces the rhythm: this is the discovery → strategy → design & build → launch sequence, presented as a chapter index.

The section is rendered as a **vertical timeline**. Anchored to the left margin, four enormous **Roman numerals** in Cinzel — `I`, `II`, `III`, `IV` — sized around 96 pixels, in obsidian. Each numeral spaces vertically with generous padding — maybe 200 pixels between centers.

Connecting the numerals: a single **thin gold vertical rule**. As the visitor scrolls down, the rule **draws itself** — from zero length at the top to full length at the bottom — at exactly the rate the visitor scrolls. This is one of the signature moments of the page. The gold line *follows the visitor down the page*, connecting the phases as they're revealed.

To the right of each numeral, the phase content:
- Phase name in Cinzel sized at 28 pixels (`Discovery`, `Strategy`, `Design & Build`, `Launch & Optimize`)
- Two to three lines of body sans describing what the phase is
- One **deliverable callout** in small caps below the description (e.g., `DELIVERABLE — A written strategy doc within 5 days`)
- One small custom **line-illustration icon** — drawn in by the same scroll-driven animation as the gold rule

When the visitor's viewport centers on a phase, its **Roman numeral fills with gold**. The phases above and below fade to 60% opacity. The active phase is the only thing on the page at full saturation. It is what the visitor is reading right now.

As they scroll past, the numeral returns to obsidian, the previous numeral fades, and the next becomes the focus.

Hovering a phase reveals a small expansion of its descriptor — an extra line or two appearing below — with a cursor change to `+`. This is a discoverable detail, not a primary interaction.

The process is **specific**. Each deliverable is concrete (a named output, a named timeframe). Concreteness > poetry in this section.

## Frame VIII — The Evidence *(Proof)*

The most dramatic tonal shift on the page: the background becomes **obsidian**. Total. The cream and ivory of the previous sections give way to a high-contrast environment that says: pay attention. This is the receipt.

The top half of the section presents **three or four metric tiles** in a horizontal row. Each tile is centered in its column. The number is enormous — Cinzel at 96 pixels or larger — in **ivory**. Below each number, a small descriptor in body sans, tracked-out, in muted ivory: `organic traffic gained · 6 months`, `engagement-to-inquiry rate`, `average project delivery`, etc.

As the section enters view, the numbers **count up from zero** to their final values — staggered, 80 milliseconds between each, with eased animation. The visitor watches results materialize in front of them. The numbers feel like museum stat panels — precise, declarative, real.

Below the metric tiles, a thin **gold rule** stretches across the center of the viewport. Below the rule:

The **anchor testimonial**. A single pull-quote, centered, in Cinzel at around 36 pixels. Three to five lines maximum. As the section enters view, the quote letters fade in left-to-right with a slight **blur-to-clear** effect — almost cinematic. Just before the text resolves, a large **opening quote-mark in gold** materializes — sized larger than the text itself, almost overlapping the first line. It is a flourish, but a disciplined one.

Below the quote, attribution: client name in body sans bold, role and business beneath, a small **circular client photo** or business logo to the left of the name. Real, named, sourced. No "Customer #43" placeholders.

Above all of this, the obsidian background has a subtle vertical light gradient that drifts very slowly as the visitor scrolls — barely perceptible, but adding depth. The room is alive.

## Frame IX — The Footprint *(Florida Coverage)*

The page transitions back to **cream**, then immediately introduces a structural shift: a two-column composition, left and right.

On the **left half** of the viewport, a **custom-drawn Florida outline** — line-drawn in obsidian on cream, with hairline weight. It is hand-illustrated, not from Google Maps. Inside the outline, small **dots** — perhaps 8 to 10 of them — mark the cities served. The dots are small, almost decorative.

On the **right half**, a vertical roster of city names in body sans large (24 pixels), one per line. Each entry: city name in obsidian on the left, a small meta line in tracked-out small caps on the right (e.g., `Miami · South FL`, `Jacksonville · Northeast FL`). A thin gold rule separates each entry.

The anchor for the section sits above both columns: `Florida-Based · Statewide` in Cinzel at 32 pixels.

As the visitor scrolls into view, the dots on the map **illuminate in gold sequentially** — 60-millisecond stagger between each. The city names on the right fade in with the same stagger, paired by row. The map is being populated in front of the visitor's eyes.

When the visitor hovers a city name on the right, its corresponding **map dot pulses gold** at a slightly larger size. Hovering a dot on the map reverses the relationship: the city name on the right gains a gold underline. The two columns are aware of each other.

The cursor over any city reads `Serving` in small caps. This is a quiet personalization — the cursor itself confirming the studio covers the visitor's market.

Below the columns, a single closing line in body sans: `Not in Florida? Selective remote engagements considered.` It preserves national optionality without diluting the Florida focus. Geographic specificity is felt-trust.

## Frame X — From the Studio *(Clarisa's Letter)*

A return to **cream** — but warmer, almost ivory-tinted. This is the most human moment of the entire page.

The composition is a two-column editorial spread, modeled on a luxury magazine's "Letter from the Editor."

On the **left**, a single **portrait of Clarisa Zuniga** — photographic, commissioned, treated in black and white. The portrait is sized to occupy roughly 40% of the viewport width. The framing is natural — three-quarter gaze, neutral background, light from one side. It is not a stock headshot. It is taken with intent.

As the portrait enters view, it **scales gently** from 1.04 to 1.0 over 800 milliseconds, with an accompanying opacity fade. The visitor watches her settle into the page.

On the **right**, the letter. Above it, a small meta line in tracked-out small caps: `FROM THE STUDIO · FLORIDA`. Below that, three to four short paragraphs in body sans, sized at 18 pixels. The voice is first-person — `I`. The paragraphs fade in with a 200-millisecond stagger.

At the close of the letter, the typed name in Cinzel at 28 pixels: `Clarisa Zuniga`. Below the typed name, a **handwritten signature SVG** — scanned from her real signature — draws itself in on scroll, the SVG path animating from zero to full over 800 milliseconds. The letter is being signed in front of the visitor.

When the visitor hovers the portrait, the black-and-white treatment softens into a low-saturation color — a quiet, restrained "she's real" moment. The reverse on mouse-out is identical.

This section is the conversion-relationship pivot of the entire page. After reading the letter, "Start a Project" no longer feels like submitting a form. It feels like writing to Clarisa.

## Frame XI — Field Notes *(Journal Teaser)*

The page returns to **ivory** for a moment of editorial restraint. This is the journal teaser. It is the smallest section on the homepage, and the most disciplined.

A single Cinzel anchor at the top: `Field Notes`. Below it, three article preview cards in a horizontal row.

Each card has **no image**. None. The discipline is total. Each card shows:
- A small meta line at the top: date and category in tracked-out small caps (e.g., `MAY 2026 · ON CRAFT`)
- The article title in medium Cinzel, sized around 24 pixels — two lines maximum
- A two-line excerpt in body sans
- A single link below: `Read →`

The cards reveal on scroll with a 100-millisecond stagger and a slight upward lift.

When the visitor hovers a card, a **gold underline** appears beneath the article title, and a thin gold rule extends from the card's right edge into the gutter — a small architectural detail that signals: this is the opening of a longer piece. The cursor reads `Read` in small caps.

Below the row of cards, a single quiet link: `All Field Notes →`. It leads to the journal index.

This section is small, disciplined, and rare. Empty or stale, it would be worse than no journal at all. It exists only if it is maintained.

## Frame XII — The Invitation *(Closing)*

The page commits to **obsidian** for the final time. Full viewport. The closing statement is about to land.

In the dead center of the viewport, an enormous **Cinzel statement** begins to write itself, letter by letter — the same technique as the hero, but slower. Each letter masks in from below with a 50-millisecond stagger. The statement is one line, possibly two. It is sized as large as Cinzel will tolerate while remaining legible — clamp(64px, 8vw, 120px). The total reveal takes about two seconds. This is the period at the end of the homepage's sentence.

Beneath the statement, after a full beat of held silence, the CTAs **fade in** — primary `Start a Project` in solid gold, secondary `Schedule a Conversation` outlined. They sit side by side. They are the only options.

Below the CTAs, a small meta line in tracked-out small caps: `clarisa@zunigastudio.com · Florida, USA`. The email is linked. The address is real. The closing is human.

In the background, almost invisibly, a soft **gold radial gradient pulse** cycles every eight seconds. It peaks at about 4% opacity. The visitor doesn't see it. They feel the room is breathing.

When the cursor enters either CTA, the **disc fills with gold** and expands — a haptic-feeling response. The button itself reacts to being approached.

The closing is not a billboard. It is an invitation, restrained and confident. The studio is asking, *quietly*, for the next step.

## Frame XIII — The Final Room *(Footer)*

Below the closing, the footer begins. Same obsidian background — there is no tonal break between closing and footer. The visitor has crossed into the final room of the experience.

The footer is **substantial**. Four columns, generously spaced:

- **Column 1:** The wordmark `Zuniga Studio` (Cinzel), a brief one-line tagline beneath it, the Florida location, the email
- **Column 2:** Services — five links, one per offering, each leading to a dedicated `/services/` page
- **Column 3:** Florida cities — six to ten city links, each leading to a future `/florida/[city]` page
- **Column 4:** Studio nav — `Work`, `Approach`, `Journal`, `Contact`

Below the columns, a thin **gold rule** stretches the full width.

Below the rule, the bottom strip: copyright on the left, the line `Designed and built in Florida` in the center, legal links (`Privacy`, `Terms`) on the right. All in body sans, tracked-out, small. The footer feels like the colophon of a book.

When the visitor hovers any footer link, a **gold underline writes itself** from left to right beneath the link. Two hundred milliseconds. Restrained. The footer is the quietest motion zone on the entire page.

The newsletter signup, if added, lives in Column 1 as a single line with a thin gold ring on focus.

## After the Walk — On the Felt-Quality

What the visitor takes away from this experience is not a memory of any single section. It is a memory of *quality* — the felt sense that they were in a place built with deliberate intention from beginning to end.

They will not be able to articulate that the typography behaves with discipline, that the color palette is restrained to a fixed budget, that no animation exists without purpose, or that the cursor responded to them at exactly the right level of subtlety. They will simply *feel* it.

That feeling is the thesis of the page. Florida small-business owners — contractors, therapists, home-service companies — have spent years browsing template-driven, busy, conversion-clichéd agency websites. They have learned to tune out the visual noise. When they land on Zuniga Studio, the absence of that noise is itself the message.

The homepage's job is not to convince visitors that the studio is good. It is to *demonstrate* what good looks like, and then to let visitors decide whether they want that for themselves.

Every section of this concept has been built to that purpose:
- §01 declares the studio's category by tone, not by claim
- §02 establishes trust quietly
- §03 takes a position
- §04 shows that the offerings are thought through, not listed
- §05 proves execution
- §06 demystifies the process
- §07 puts results on record
- §08 confirms geographic relevance
- §09 introduces Clarisa as a real human
- §10 signals ongoing intellectual seriousness
- §11 closes with an invitation, not a pitch
- §13 anchors the page in real contact and real legal substance

By the time the visitor reaches the bottom — if they reach the bottom — they have been walked through a complete experience. They know what the studio believes, what it offers, how it works, who runs it, and how to engage. They have been shown, not told.

The "Start a Project" CTA is present at the top of the nav, in the hero, beneath the manifesto, on every service card, on every case study, in the closing, and in the footer. It has been the through-line of the page. By the time the visitor reaches it for the final time, the decision to click has become small.

That is what we are designing.

## What this concept commits us to

If approved, the following constraints carry through every subsequent deliverable:

- **Typography:** Cinzel reserved for monumental moments only. Body sans-serif (TBD — Söhne, Inter Display, or similar; selected during type specimen phase) handles all body and small-caps work. Cinzel never below 24px.
- **Color budget:** Obsidian and ivory are the structural binary. Cream warms transitions. Gold is the accent budget — never more than one gold element on screen unless intentionally stacked. Bronze handles hover states only.
- **Motion vocabulary:** Approximately 15 distinct motions across the entire page. Every motion serves orientation, meaning, or reward. No decorative animation. No autoplay. All motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Imagery rule:** No stock photography anywhere. Custom SVG line illustrations for icons. Real photography for portraits and case studies, treated with magazine discipline (B&W or single-tone). The custom Florida map is drawn, never embedded.
- **Voice convention:** First-person `I` for the founder's letter. `Zuniga Studio` as institutional voice elsewhere. `We` used sparingly and only in established-discipline contexts. Never fabricates team scale.
- **Performance budget:** LCP under 1.5 seconds. INP under 100 milliseconds. CLS under 0.05. Cinzel preloaded. Single web font. No icon fonts.
- **Accessibility floor:** AA contrast minimum. Custom cursor augments, never replaces, the system pointer. Keyboard navigation reaches every CTA with a visible gold focus ring.
